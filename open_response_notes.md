# Open-Field Response Notes

Source: `data/participant_data_cleaned.csv`

## Response Counts

- `open_trust`: 15 usable responses
- `open_explanation`: 15 usable responses
- `open_improvement`: 12 usable responses

Blank responses in the cleaned dataset are marked as `no_response` and excluded from the qualitative theme counts.

## Main Themes

Participants mostly evaluated trust through whether the AI recommendation matched their own reasoning. The strongest trust theme was that the answer made logical sense or agreed with the intuitive answer. This appeared in about 8 of 15 trust responses. Participants described the AI as trustworthy when it had sound reasoning, singled out the most urgent task, or explained its reasoning clearly.

Confidence wording mattered. Several participants reacted negatively to confidence language in both directions. In the uncertainty-calibrated condition, words like "moderately" or mild confidence made the AI feel less trustworthy to some participants. In the high-confidence condition, one participant said `"Clearly" is a red flag`, and another said the recommendation needed more explanation. This suggests confidence language can backfire when it feels unsupported.

Explanation quality was mixed but generally valued. About 9 of 15 explanation responses described the explanation as helpful, clear, appropriate, or coherent. However, some participants found explanations excessive or redundant, while others found them insufficient. The ideal explanation seems to be concise but evidence-backed.

A few participants expressed broader AI skepticism. One participant said they would never use AI in that situation, while another said they would need more data before trusting it. These responses suggest baseline attitudes toward AI may strongly shape interpretation of the recommendation, independent of condition.

## Improvement Themes

The most common improvement request was for more context or reasoning depth. Participants asked for more data points, examples, conditional reasoning, what-if scenarios, or a sentence explaining the table/task context. This was especially important when the AI used strong confidence or gave a minimal recommendation.

One participant made a useful study-design suggestion: ask participants for their decision first, then show the AI recommendation, then ask whether they would change their answer. That would measure AI influence more directly than asking only after exposure.

## Interpretation

The open responses suggest that trust was not driven simply by longer explanations or stronger confidence. Participants trusted the AI when its recommendation felt logically grounded, aligned with the visible task constraints, and did not overclaim. Unsupported certainty and vague uncertainty both created skepticism.

Suggested report language:

> Participants tended to trust AI recommendations when they were concise, logically justified, and aligned with their own interpretation of task urgency and importance. However, confidence language was sensitive: strong claims such as "clearly" and hedged claims such as "moderately confident" both reduced trust for some participants when not paired with sufficient reasoning.
